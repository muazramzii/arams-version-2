§

Two blockers the data raises against the locked decisions

Neither makes a locked decision technically impossible, so I have designed around both rather than stopping. Both need your answer before Phase 3.

Blocker 1D1 leaves 77 lecturers with no one who can validate their work

D1 removes the Admin fallback. TDPP is now the only validator. I checked TDPP coverage against the lecturer population:

faculty lecturers TDPP research records FKAAS 77 0 77 ← no validator exists FSKTM 8 1 58 FKMP 4 1 22 FKAAB 4 1 64 FPTV 3 1 40 FKEE 2 1 17

FKAAS is the largest faculty in the system by lecturer count and has no TDPP appointment. Under D1 as written, any submission from those 77 accounts enters a queue nobody can act on. It does not bite today only because all 77 are inactive shell accounts — the moment faculty IT activates them, 77 lecturers are stranded.

This is an appointment gap, not an architecture flaw, and the fix is administrative: appoint a TDPP for FKAAS. But the architecture must make the gap impossible to miss rather than silently swallowing submissions, so the model does three things:

  • TDPP becomes a dated appointment (faculty_leaders), not a profile row. A faculty with no active appointment is a queryable state, not an absence.
  • A faculty may hold more than one concurrent TDPP appointment — deputies, or cover during leave — so a single person is never a single point of failure.
  • Submission is refused at the point of submission, with a clear message, if the lecturer's faculty has no active validator. Better a lecturer is told immediately than a record sits unreviewed for months.

Needed from you: confirm that refusing submission is the right behaviour, and that FKAAS will receive a TDPP appointment before those accounts are activated. I have not introduced an Admin fallback — D1 is respected.

Blocker 2D4 needs an effective date that 88 existing records do not have

D4 makes the record's own effective date the basis for KPI credit. That requires every research record to have one. Checking availability across the real data:

entity rows usable effective date publication 107 107 ✓ pub_year research income 72 72 ✓ year_received award 31 31 ✓ award_year h-index 87 87 ✓ record_year (metric, not research — see D2) grant 71 1 ✗ 70 have NULL start_date IP record 18 0 ✗ filing_date AND grant_date are both 100% NULL

Every IP record in the system has no date of any kind, and 70 of 71 grants have no start date. Under a strict NOT NULL effective_date, 88 records cannot be migrated at all.

Design accommodation. research_records carries effective_date as NULL-able alongside a mandatory effective_date_precision of DAY, MONTH, YEAR, or UNKNOWN. A record with precision UNKNOWN is valid, visible, and counted in totals — but it is excluded from every period-scoped KPI calculation and every time-series chart, and it appears in an Admin "records needing a date" worklist. Nothing is silently dropped and nothing is silently guessed.

New records submitted through ARAMS 2.0 require a real date; UNKNOWN is reachable only by migration.

Needed from you: confirm this, and confirm that backfilling the 70 grant start dates and 18 IP filing dates from UTHM records is owned by the research office. Until that lands, grant and IP KPI targets cannot be scored.

Everything else in D1–D6 holds cleanly

D2 — H-Index leaves the workflow entirely; 87 rows become metric snapshots and 87 no-op approvals disappear. D3 — the 67 bundled submissions split deterministically; one-to-one is enforced by a UNIQUE constraint, not by convention. D5 — benchmarking is a separate read model with a minimum-cohort rule. D6 — adding a research type is one reference row plus one subtype table, with no change to submissions, KPI, audit, or analytics.

One D5 detail worth flagging early: with only six faculties holding data, an "institution median" computed from a cohort of two or three is re-identifiable — a TDPP could infer another faculty's exact figure. Section 5 handles this with a minimum-cohort threshold.

§

Design principles

  1. Every entity earns its place. No table exists because a screen exists. Each one below is justified by a business fact, a locked decision, or a defect in the current data.
  2. Integrity is structural, not procedural. Where 1.0 relied on the application remembering a rule, 2.0 uses a constraint. The empty-string ENUM corruption, the duplicate grant codes, and the one-to-many submission bundling are all fixed by constraints rather than by discipline.
  3. Vocabularies are data with foreign keys. The University/Universiti split and the 52 blank report_type values both trace to vocabularies living in JavaScript. Reference tables with FKs make those states unrepresentable.
  4. Workflow state and domain data stay apart. A research record is a fact. A submission is a process about that fact. They are different tables with different lifetimes.
  5. History is append-only. Reviews, affiliations, appointments, and audit events are never updated in place. Current state is derived from history, not substituted for it.
  6. Denormalize only deliberately, and say so. Three denormalizations appear below. Each is named, justified, and given a reconciliation job.
  7. MySQL 8 in strict mode, InnoDB, utf8mb4_0900_ai_ci. Non-negotiable — non-strict mode is what turned invalid values into blanks.
  8. Surrogate BIGINT UNSIGNED primary keys throughout, with natural keys enforced as UNIQUE alongside. Business identifiers change; primary keys must not.

3.1 · One profile table, not three

1.0 stored a person's name and contact details in tbl_lecturer, tbl_tdpp, or tbl_admin depending on their role. That is why a TDPP cannot have a profile page, why api/update_user.php blindly writes to two tables and misses the third, and why the same attribute exists three times.

staff_profiles holds every person once. Role stays on users. The data confirms this is safe: all five TDPP accounts have role = 'TDPP' and no lecturer profile, so the merge is a straight union with no collisions.

4.1 · The attribution rule

Two different questions need two different answers, and 1.0 conflated them:

QuestionResolved fromChanges on transfer?
Which faculty does this lecturer belong to now?Open staff_affiliations rowYes — correctly
Which faculty gets credit for this 2020 publication?research_records.attributed_faculty_id, frozen at approvalNo — correctly
Which TDPP reviews this submission?Affiliation at submission timeFollows the lecturer
Which faculty's queue does a pending item sit in?submissions.faculty_id_at_submissionFrozen at submission

Attribution is computed once and frozen. When a submission reaches APPROVED, the system resolves the lecturer's primary affiliation as at the record's effective_date and writes it to research_records.attributed_faculty_id, with attributed_at and attribution_basis alongside.

Denormalization 1 of 3 — and why it is right

attributed_faculty_id is derivable from affiliations plus effective_date, so storing it violates 3NF. I am doing it deliberately for three reasons: a published report saying "FSKTM produced 37 publications in 2020" must not silently change because someone later corrects an affiliation record; recomputing attribution across a five-way join on every analytics query is the wrong cost; and freezing it makes attribution auditable — you can see what was decided and when.

Mitigation: a nightly reconciliation job recomputes expected attribution and reports divergence to Admin rather than silently correcting it. Divergence is a signal that someone edited history, which is exactly what an Admin should be told about.

Records with effective_date_precision = 'UNKNOWN' — the 88 grants and IP records — fall back to the affiliation at submission date, flagged attribution_basis = 'SUBMISSION_DATE_FALLBACK' so the approximation is visible rather than assumed.

Migration: one open affiliation row per lecturer from today's faculty_id, then the four tbl_faculty_transfer rows replayed in timestamp order to close and open the intervening rows. Rozlini's 37 records recover attributed_faculty_id = 1 (FSKTM) from the old snapshot column that nothing ever read.

Research record architecture

The central structural decision, serving D3 and D6 together.

5.1 · Supertype and subtypes

Every research record is a row in research_records plus exactly one row in a subtype table. This is class-table inheritance, and it is what makes one-to-one submissions enforceable and new research types cheap.

"Isn't this just tbl_research_data again?"

It is a fair challenge, since Phase 1 recommended removing that table. The difference is what the parent carries.

tbl_research_data was a workflow row pretending to be a parent: it held status, remarks, reviewer, and soft-delete flags; it had no identity of its own; nothing queried it for ownership; and it permitted one-to-many children — which is how 67 bundles came to exist.

research_records is a domain supertype: it holds only what is genuinely true of every research record — owner, type, title, effective date, attributed faculty, soft delete — and carries no workflow state at all. Workflow lives in submissions, which references it one-to-one through a UNIQUE constraint.

The practical payoff: KPI contributions, audit events, and notifications all reference one stable table instead of five polymorphic targets; "all research owned by this lecturer" is one indexed query instead of a five-way UNION; and the effective-date and attribution rules that D4 and historical attribution depend on are defined once rather than five times, which is precisely how they drifted in 1.0.

5.2 · Adding a research type later — the D6 test

The architecture must let Research Projects or Postgraduate Supervision be added without restructuring. Concretely, adding one requires:

  1. One row in research_types.
  2. One subtype table with research_record_id as its primary key.
  3. Reference rows for any new vocabulary.
  4. One Form Request, one Resource, one form component.

Unchanged: submissions, the state machine, reviews, revisions, KPI targets and contributions, audit, notifications, soft delete, attribution, and every existing analytics query. That is the test D6 asks the architecture to pass.

5.3 · Contributors

publication_authors and ip_inventors break the 1NF violation in the current comma-separated authors and inventors fields. Columns: research_record_id, staff_profile_id (NULL for external people), person_name, author_order, is_corresponding, is_student, affiliation_text. The original string is preserved verbatim in publications.raw_authors so nothing is lost in migration.

This is what makes internal co-authorship, correct fractional credit across faculties, and honest duplicate detection possible. Today every duplicate in the data belongs to a single lecturer, so the question has not yet arisen — it will the moment a second faculty is active.

5.4 · Benchmarking and the small-cohort problem (D5)

benchmark_cohorts defines which comparisons a TDPP may see, with a min_cohort_size guard. With six faculties holding data, an "institution median" over a cohort of two lets a TDPP subtract their own figure and read the other faculty's exactly.

Rule: a benchmark value is returned only when the cohort excluding the requester contains at least three faculties with non-zero data for that measure and period. Below that, the API returns a suppressed marker and the UI reads "not enough faculties reporting to compare". Enforced in the analytics service, not in the frontend.

6

Research subtypes

6.1 · Publications

publications: research_record_id (PK, FK, CASCADE), journal_name, issn, pub_year, volume, issue, pages, publication_type_id, author_role_id, quartile, impact_factor, doi, url, country_id, student_author, national_collaboration, international_collaboration, industries_collaboration, raw_authors.

publication_indexings replaces the SET column. This is not cosmetic — it is the structural fix for the KPI defect where indexing_type = 'Scopus' silently missed all four publications indexed 'Scopus,WoS'. As a join table, "is indexed in Scopus" becomes an EXISTS, which cannot fail that way.

effective_date derives from pub_year with precision YEAR. All 107 rows migrate cleanly.

6.2 · Grants — project versus participation

This is the second significant structural change, and the real data forces it. Grant Q940 appears three times, once per participating lecturer; ten other codes appear twice. Under 1.0 these are three unrelated grants worth three times the money.

TableRepresentsKey columns
grant_projectsThe institutional grant — one per award, sharedgrant_code UNIQUE, title, funder_id, grant_category_id, grant_level_id, total_amount, start_date, end_date, status_id, mygrants_id

UNIQUE (grant_project_id, research_record_id → owner) makes it impossible for one lecturer to claim the same grant twice, which is exactly the duplicate pattern in the data. Institutional funding totals sum grant_projects.total_amount and stop triple-counting; individual credit uses participation and role.

D3 is respected: each lecturer's participation is one research record and one submission, reviewed independently by their own faculty's TDPP — which is correct, since two participants may sit in different faculties.

Migration risk: merging Q940's three rows assumes they really are one grant. This must be verified against UTHM records before the merge, not after. Marked High in the migration plan.

6.3 · Research income

research_incomes: research_record_id (PK), grant_project_id (NULL, FK — now pointing at the project rather than a participation), income_category_id, source_name, amount DECIMAL(15,2), currency CHAR(3) DEFAULT 'MYR', year_received, received_on (NULL).

70 of 72 rows already link to a grant and none exceeds its parent's amount, so this relationship is sound and migrates directly. A CHECK constraint enforces amount > 0. Effective date comes from year_received, precision YEAR — all 72 rows migrate cleanly.

6.4 · IP records

ip_records: research_record_id (PK), ip_type_id, ip_number, country_id, filing_date, grant_date, registration_status_id, raw_inventors. UNIQUE on ip_number where not null.

Effective date prefers filing_date, falling back to grant_date. All 18 existing rows have neither, so all 18 migrate with precision UNKNOWN and land on the Admin backfill worklist. This is Blocker 2 in concrete form.

6.5 · Awards

awards: research_record_id (PK), award_type_id, organiser, award_level_id, award_year. Per the locked assumption, awards become a full research record inside the submission workflow — they already feed KPI, and unvalidated data must not.

The 31 existing rows currently attach straight to a lecturer with no workflow. They migrate as records with a synthetic APPROVED submission marked origin = 'MIGRATED_V1' and decided_by = NULL, so their unvalidated provenance stays visible rather than being laundered into a clean approval. All 31 have award_year, so effective dates are fine.

7.1 · Immutable review history

submission_reviews: id, submission_id, revision_no, reviewer_user_id, reviewer_role, decision (APPROVED · REJECTED · REVISION_REQUESTED), remarks, decided_at, origin. Insert only — no UPDATE or DELETE path exists in the application, and the database user has no rights to either on this table.

submission_revisions stores a JSON snapshot of the record as submitted at each revision, so "what did the reviewer actually see when they rejected version 1?" has an answer. UNIQUE (submission_id, revision_no).

D1 enforcement — three layers, no Admin fallback

  1. Policy. SubmissionPolicy::review() returns true only when the actor's role is TDPP and they hold an active faculty_leaders appointment for faculty_id_at_submission and they are not the submitter.
  2. Data. reviewer_role permits TDPP for any row with origin = 'ARAMS_2'. ADMIN_LEGACY exists solely to carry 1.0 history and is rejected on insert for new rows.
  3. Absence. There is no admin-approval endpoint, no auto-approve path, and no bypass. api/admin_add_record.php is not carried forward. An Admin creating a record on a lecturer's behalf creates it as DRAFT owned by that lecturer, who must submit it themselves.

7.2 · State transitions as data

submission_transitions is a small reference table — from_status, to_status, required_role, requires_remarks, label — read by the state machine. Keeping transitions as rows rather than as if statements means the legal moves are inspectable, testable, and auditable in one place.

9.1 · How credit is calculated

  1. A submission reaches APPROVED. A domain event fires with the research_record_id.
  2. The record's effective_date is read. If precision is UNKNOWN, processing stops — the record cannot be placed in a period and is listed for backfill.
  3. Candidate targets are those whose kpi_periods range contains the effective date, whose measure matches the research type, and whose scope covers the owner.
  4. Criteria are evaluated from kpi_target_criteria. contains is used for set-valued facts like indexing, which is why the Scopus/WoS defect cannot recur.
  5. A kpi_contributions row is written. Progress is recomputed by summing contributions, never incremented.
  6. If the record is later withdrawn, superseded, or soft-deleted, its contribution is deleted and progress recomputes downward.

D4 in one line

A publication dated December 2026 and approved in January 2027 credits period 2026. Approval date determines when it becomes visible; effective date determines which period it belongs to. Validation delay can no longer distort performance.

Why kpi_contributions matters. It is the difference between a progress number and an auditable one. A lecturer can see exactly which records counted; a TDPP can see why a target was met; and recomputation is idempotent because progress is derived rather than accumulated. The 1.0 counter that read 19 against a target of 1 is unrepresentable here.

Migration: the 12 institutional targets migrate directly. The 9 tasks migrate as staff-scope targets plus assignments, with progress reset to zero and recomputed under the effective-date rule. Their existing progress values are not salvageable — they were computed under the lifetime-counting rule D4 replaces, so carrying them forward would import the defect.

Audit architecture

One append-only table. audit_events: id, actor_user_id (NULL for system), actor_role, action (ENUM), auditable_type, auditable_id, changes (JSON before/after), context (JSON), ip_address, user_agent, created_at.

Three changes from 1.0, each addressing a measured defect: actions are a typed enum, not free text — today's log contains the typo Rejectd Submission; coverage is enforced by emitting from the service layer, not from controllers — today 7 audit rows exist against 272 approvals; and changes records what actually changed, which 1.0 never captured, so an Admin edit to an approved record is now reconstructable.

auditable_type / auditable_id is intentionally polymorphic with no FK. Audit rows must survive the deletion of what they describe, so referential integrity would be actively wrong here. This is the one place polymorphism is the right answer.

Not audited: page views, reads, or anything derivable. Today's log is 56% "Generated Report" noise while missing 265 of 272 approvals — coverage should follow significance, not convenience.

Notification architecture

The routing fix. Email is queued, never sent inside the request. 1.0 sends mail synchronously inside the approval handler, so a slow SMTP server delays the reviewer's response.

Soft-delete strategy

Locked assumption 6 — deletion and workflow status are orthogonal.

1.0 let a record be simultaneously Approved and is_deleted = 1, and two rows already are. Nothing defined what that meant. Here the two axes are formally independent and their interaction is specified.

TableSoft deleteRationale
research_recordsYesCascades logically to its subtype and submission
staff_profiles, usersYesDeactivation must not orphan authored research
grant_projects, research_groups, facultiesYesReferenced by history
submissionsNoDerives visibility from its record. Deleting workflow separately is meaningless
submission_reviews, submission_revisions, audit_eventsNoImmutable history. Deleting a decision defeats the purpose
kpi_contributionsNoHard-deleted on recompute — derived data, not a fact
hindex_snapshotsYesCorrections happen; the superseded reading stays visible

Interaction rules

  1. A soft-deleted record is excluded from analytics, KPI, and reports regardless of workflow status. Deletion always wins.
  2. Deleting a record does not change its submission status. An approved-then-deleted record stays APPROVED and deleted — the approval is a historical fact.
  3. Deletion removes the record's kpi_contributions and triggers recompute, so KPI progress falls.
  4. Deletion requires a reason and writes an audit event with the pre-deletion state.
  5. Restoration is an Admin action, audited, and triggers recompute.
  6. Only Admin may delete an approved record. A lecturer may delete only a DRAFT.
  7. Hard deletion exists nowhere in the application. Purging is a documented DBA procedure with a retention policy.

The two existing rows (data_id 88 and 212, both Approved and deleted by admin user 1 on 2026-06-14) migrate as APPROVED + soft-deleted, with deletion_reason = 'Migrated from ARAMS 1.0 — original reason not recorded'. Both have matching Admin Deleted Publication audit entries, so the actor is known even though the reason is not.
